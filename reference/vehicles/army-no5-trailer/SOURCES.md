# Australian Army No. 5 trailer

Game id: `no5` (`no5Trailer` in `src/data/trailers.ts`, drawn by `buildNo5` in `src/render/trailerView.ts`).

**What to match.** Trailer, Cargo, ½ tonne, No. 5: the Army's steel box trailer on one axle, built for towing behind all its Land Rovers from the mid-1960s and still towed behind the Perentie. A deep box with a rolled lip round the top, pressed ribs and rope hooks on the sides, the wheel arches inside the box so the wheels tuck under its edges, a fixed rear panel with two round red reflectors, lamps in the bottom corners and the plate between them, and a single-tube drawbar to a lunette ring for the pintle hook, with a prop leg that swings up and, on the later trailers, a spare wheel standing on the drawbar. Flat Army green. Leave out camper conversions, tents, extra boxes and tarps: the game draws it bare.

## Figures

The overall and inside dimensions and the payload are from Stephen Stansfield's guide as quoted on REMLR's No. 5 page ([remlr.com/trailer-no5.html](https://www.remlr.com/trailer-no5.html)) and repeated on its forum ([No. 5 trailer dimensions](https://remlr.com/forum/index.php?topic=1187.0)). REMLR's pages refused automated access (a bot check), so these were read from search results quoting them, not from the page itself. The tare is one owner's weighbridge figure when registering one ([Registration of Army No 5 ½ tonne Cargo Trailer](https://remlr.com/forum/index.php?topic=1220.0)); the same thread notes it has no brakes.

**Check these first.** The model is about 80 mm narrower overall than the published 1,550 mm: the lip may stand further out, or the hubs may sit wider. The floor height and the tyre size are judged from the photographs (the tyre's top meets the channel along the bottom of the box). How the prop leg folds, and the spare wheel's bracket, are guesses from one photograph of an upturned trailer.
