# Nissan Patrol 160 series, MQ short-wheelbase hardtop

Game id: `mq`. Body: `src/render/mqBody.ts`, with `mqGear.ts` (bull bar, rear bar, spare carrier, underside, rack, wheels), `mqInterior.ts` and `mqPaint.ts`. Tuning: `src/data/mq.ts`.

**What to match.** An Australian 1980–83 MQ Patrol (Nissan's 160 series) short-wheelbase two-door hardtop before the 1983 MK facelift: the round headlamps at the ends of a black grille panel with NISSAN across it, the painted valance with the indicators under it, a flat bonnet level with the guard tops and the door belt line, squared wheel openings with pressed lips, a near-upright screen, the hardtop behind the B-pillar with one long sliding window each side and a single side-hinged rear door, upright tail lamps on the rear corners. Under it the SD33 3.3 L diesel six, the four-speed, a two-speed transfer and leaf springs on both live axles, 7.50R16 tyres on steel wheels. The bull bar with the winch, the rear bar with the tow ball, the gutter rack with the second spare and the planks, and the swing-away spare carrier on the rear bar behind the door are added for the game. The mid blue paint and the white side stripe are a choice for the game: the Australian MQ was sold in bright colours with a stripe kit, and no photograph of an Australian car in good light was found.

The body behind the A-pillar did not change with the 1983 facelift (MK in Australia), so the Dutch and German hardtops of 1986–87 with rectangular lamps are the same panels from the windscreen back. Spanish-built Patrols (Motor Ibérica) kept the round-lamp front, which is why the clearest front and rear three-quarters are of a Spanish-plated 1983 car. Leave out what the photographs say is local or aftermarket: the Spanish car's green stripe kit and bull bar, the Dutch cars' alloy wheels and twin spares, the German car's snorkel, and the high-roof vans' taller tops.

The heights that set the side, read off the Spanish three-quarters and the Dutch 1987 rear three-quarter scaled by the 0.80 m tyres, with the lift of the Dutch car taken out: bottom of the sides 0.50 m off the ground, belt line (top of the doors, the guards and the bonnet, all on one level) 1.13 m, top of the side glass 1.74 m, roof 1.88 m. Top of the wheel openings 0.88 m. Front door shut −0.62, B-pillar 0.44, hardtop seam 0.47 (all in the game's z).

**Check these first.** The model was compared with the studio renders at `studio.view(-40, 0.4, 0.2, 0, 0.4, 0.2, 8)` (side, 1600×700, 125 px/m), `studio.view(0, 1.0, -40, 0, 0.9, 0, 8)` (front) and `studio.view(0, 1.0, 40, 0, 0.9, 0, 8)` (rear) against the photographs by eye, not by overlay: no square-on side photograph of a stock car exists under a free licence, so the side was built to the published length, height and wheelbase and the heights above, and the renders were measured back (roof 1.885 m, side skin 3.98 m long, door bottom 0.44 m). The track is an estimate. The grille panel's height, the headlamp height and the indicator positions come from the 1985 LWB wagon's square-on front; the hardtop's roof step at the B-pillar from the Spanish rear three-quarter. The rear door's split of handle, badge and plate (all on the left, clear of the spare) was laid out round the game's spare carrier and is not checked against a photograph of the factory layout.

**The first spare (since v1.60).** It hangs on a swing-away carrier bolted to the rear bar, not to the door skin, as the MQ hardtop's carrier was: a post on a foot plate at the bar's right end (x 0.82, outboard of the right tail lamp), the frame hung on it by two knuckles, an upper arm over the lamp and a lower brace under it to the wheel plate, and a latch arm down to a pin post on the bar at x −0.2. The wheel's axle points backwards: hub at x 0.22, y 0.56 (1.08 m off the ground), z `TAIL` + 0.27 (`MQ_SPARE` in `mqGear.ts`); its inner hub cap sits on the carrier's backing plate 0.12 m off the door skin, its inner sidewall 0.155 m off, its outer face (the wing nut on the centre stud) 0.45 m behind the tail. Behind it the whole door stays visible from the side: the handle, badge, plate, both hinges and both lamps. Before v1.60 the wheel was placed unturned, its axle across the car, so its tread ran 0.4 m into the door and the load space. `rear-34-right-hardtop-1986-spares.jpg` shows a door-mounted pair on a Dutch car; the swing-away carrier on the bar is the game's choice for a car whose door would otherwise carry 40 kg on its hinges.

## Gaps

**No photograph is committed in this folder.** Every request for an original from `upload.wikimedia.org` was answered with HTTP 429 from this container (the same block the 60 Series work met in v1.10); only thumbnails up to 1,920 px came through, which `../README.md` forbids committing. The model was built from those working copies. `manifest.json` lists each file with its Commons page and the original's URL: download them to the file names below to complete the folder, then lay the side render over the best of them.

No square-on side, no interior, engine bay or underside photograph of a 160 under a free licence was found, so the cabin follows the published trim (vinyl buckets, rubber mats, a hooded cluster of two round dials, a floor handbrake) and the 60 Series' layout. No photograph of an Australian MQ in daylight: the night shot `front-34-left-swb-australia-night.jpg` only confirms the SWB hardtop existed here with round lamps. Track, gear ratios and the fuel tank are not in any source found (`research-notes.md`).
