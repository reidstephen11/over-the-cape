# Nissan Patrol 160 series (MQ): published facts

Target for the game: Australian 1980–83 MQ short-wheelbase hardtop, round headlamps, SD33 diesel.

Facts in my own words, with the URL each came from. Sources disagree in places and those disagreements are kept, not resolved. Confidence labels as in `../landcruiser-60/research-notes.md`:

- **A**: two independent sources agree, or a source quoting a Nissan document.
- **B**: one reasonable source (a spec database or an enthusiast reference).
- **C**: forum post, third-party catalogue or a figure read off a photograph. Treat as a lead, not a fact.

No Nissan brochure, workshop manual or owner's manual was read for these notes. Several pages that would have helped could not be read from this container: carsales' 1981 SWB listing was read through a text summary only; `uniquecarsandparts.com.au`, the Off-Road.com MQ FAQ, the 80sHero MQ page and `automobile-catalog.com` refused the request (402, 403, 503 or a paywall).

## Model history

- Launched in Australia in 1980 as the MQ, replacing the 60 series. Five bodies at launch: short-wheelbase hardtop, long-wheelbase hardtop, cab-chassis, pick-up and long-wheelbase wagon (CGMQ, 4X4A). **A**
- Part-time four-wheel drive with a two-speed transfer, selectable on the move for the first time; leaf springs and live axles at both ends; separate ladder chassis; recirculating-ball steering; front discs (CGMQ, WP-EN). **A**
- 1983 facelift (MK in Australia and New Zealand, a code Nissan's own literature did not use): rectangular headlamps, upgraded front suspension, a five-speed for all but the basic commercials, the SD33T turbo diesel (WP-EN, CGMQ, 4X4A). **A** So the round-lamp car is 1980–83.
- Power steering standard and the five-speed across the range in 1986; a limited-slip rear differential came during the run; the SD33's 24 V electrics were replaced by 12 V with the turbo (4X4A, CGMQ). **B**
- Australian cars came in bright colours with "loud" graphics, optional white Sunraysia-style rims and TURBO stickers on turbo models (4X4A). **B** The game's blue and white stripe are a choice within that.

## Transmission

- Four-speed manual at launch; three-speed 3N71 automatic on the L28 LWB from about 1981; five-speed from 1983 (WP-EN, 4X4A). **A**
- Two-speed transfer, 1:1 high range; the low ratio is not given by any source found (WP-EN). The game uses 2.0: **C**.
- Rear axle H233 standard in Australia (a web summary of an enthusiast guide); the ratio is not given. The game uses 4.375 and the four-speed ratios 3.592 / 2.246 / 1.415 / 1.00, reverse 3.657, from memory of enthusiast tables: **C**, unverified.
- Fuel tank: not given by any source found. The game uses 80 L: an assumption, flagged in `src/data/mq.ts`.
