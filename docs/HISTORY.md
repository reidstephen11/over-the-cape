# History sources

The history in the game is kept to what these sources say. Where they disagree, the game leaves the disputed detail out (noted under each place). Sources were read in October 2026.

## Episode 1: the CREB Track

### The track (briefing)

- **Claim:** The Cairns Regional Electricity Board cut the CREB as the service track for the power line to Cooktown. The line now runs further inland and the track is used by four-wheel drives (and motorbikes and mountain bikes, not mentioned in the game).
- **Claim:** It opens once the wet season's damage is repaired, and the opening depends on the weather.
- **Sources:** [Cape York Weekly: Unofficial caretaker concerned about the future of CREB Track](https://capeyorkweekly.com.au/unofficial-caretaker-concerned-about-the-future-of-creb-track/7644/); [Douglas Shire Council: The CREB Track](https://douglas.qld.gov.au/the-creb-track/); [Douglas Shire Council: CREB Track opening 22 August 2020](https://douglas.qld.gov.au/creb-track-opening-22-august-2020/); [Newsport: Gearing up for a big Creb Track opening, weather permitting (June 2026)](https://www.newsport.com.au/2026/june/gearing-up-for-a-big-creb-track-opening-weather-permitting); [HEMA Maps: The CREB Track](https://hemamaps.com/blogs/location-guides/the-creb-track-cape-york-qld); [Isuzu UTE: Tropic Wonder](https://www.isuzuute.com.au/discover/featured-stories/tropic-wonder).
- **Left out:** the year the track and line were built. No source found gives it. One source says the track fell out of use when the Bloomfield Track was cut nearer the coast; another says the line moved inland. The game uses only the second.
- **Disputed:** the length (71 km, 77 km and about 50 km are all given). The game doesn't state it.

### The old line camp, the linesman's hut and the poles (side trips)

- **Claim:** none beyond the briefing's: the CREB cut the track to service the power line to Cooktown, and the line has since moved inland (sources above).
- **Game choice:** the two huts, the crates with a jerry can and tyre plugs, and the leaning poles without wires are made up for the game. No source found describes line camps or huts on the CREB, so the notes describe what is in the game, not what was there.
- **Left out:** when the line was built and when its poles came down. A search in October 2026 turned up forum posts on ExplorOz ([CREB Track](https://www.exploroz.com/forum/19143/creb-track), [Comment: CREB Track](https://www.exploroz.com/forum/89082/comment-creb-track)) saying the line was being built in the late 1960s and that most of the poles were pulled out in 2009, after the line was rerouted inland via Lakeland. The pages couldn't be opened to check them, and forum posts aren't a firm source, so the game states neither.

### Daintree Village

- **Claim:** George Elphinstone Dalrymple named the Daintree River in December 1873 after the geologist Richard Daintree, who never came to the region. Dalrymple reported red cedar; cutters followed and floated logs down the river. By 1888 the cedar was nearly gone and the trade had collapsed. The village was the cutters' base.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Daintree River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daintree_River); [Wikipedia: Daintree, Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daintree,_Queensland); [Port Douglas Magazine 44: The history of Daintree Village](https://tourismportdouglas.com.au/port-douglas-magazine/issue-44/the-history-of-daintree-village); [Destination Daintree: Daintree Village](https://www.destinationdaintree.com/daintreevillage.html); [Destination Daintree: historical timeline](https://destinationdaintree.com/the-daintree/history-culture/the-daintree-historical-timeline).
- **Disputed:** when the first settlers arrived (1879 in one timeline, "late 1870s and early 1880s" in Wikipedia). Left out.

### Daintree River crossing

- **Claim:** The crossing is wide and about a metre deep; drivers take a wide arc downstream to stay in the shallowest water. Saltwater crocodiles live in the river, so the crossing shouldn't be walked.
- **Sources:** [ExplorOz: CREB Track](https://www.exploroz.com/routes/creb-track); [Club 4X4: Travel the CREB](https://www.club4x4.com.au/travel-the-creb-bloomfield-track-loop/); [Nick and Pam: Cape York Part One (2018)](https://nickandpam.com/2018/09/11/cape-york-part-one/); [Ryan Moody Fishing: Tackling the CREB track (Part 1)](https://www.ryanmoodyfishing.com/creb-track-part-1/).
- **Game choice:** the shallow line is drawn as a gravel bar curving downstream.

### Big Red and Bob's Hill

- **Claim:** Trip guides name the steep Big Red climb as the most challenging point, with a bypass round it, and Bob's Hill, about halfway, as arguably the hardest part. Both are said "to many drivers" in the game because the guides disagree on which is worst.
- **Sources:** [The Ultimate Guide to Traveling the CREB Track (Dangerous Roads)](https://www.dangerousroads.org/australia-and-oceania/australia/8751-creb-track.html); [True North Adventures: The CREB Track](https://truenorthadventures.com.au/the-creb-track/); [Explore Cape York: The CREB Track](https://explorecapeyork.com.au/journey-to-the-tip-of-australia/creb-track/); [Tropical North Queensland: CREB Track itinerary](https://tropicalnorthqueensland.org.au/listing/itinerary/creb-track/).
- **Game choice:** the red clay going slippery in the wet and tyres being cut on rock are general four-wheel-driving advice, not claims about these hills.

### The range lookout: the 1983–84 blockade and World Heritage

- **Claim:** Douglas Shire Council began the road from Cape Tribulation to Bloomfield in 1983. Protesters blockaded the work in December 1983 and again in 1984. The road was finished, but the protest drew national attention, which led to the nomination of Queensland's wet tropical rainforests for World Heritage listing; they were inscribed in 1988, and commercial logging in them was banned.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Bloomfield Track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomfield_Track); [Wikipedia: Cape Tribulation, Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Tribulation,_Queensland); [Daintree Blockade: The Bloomfield Track](https://www.daintreeblockade.com.au/daintree-blockade-road-today.html); [ExplorOz: Bloomfield Track](https://www.exploroz.com/treks/bloomfield-track).
- **Note:** this is the coastal Bloomfield Track, not the CREB; the game tells it at the lookout because the two run side by side through the same forest.

### The concrete

- **Claim:** The last big descent towards Wujal Wujal is concreted, with cuts across it for traction, and is very steep.
- **Sources:** [Tropical North Queensland: CREB Track itinerary](https://tropicalnorthqueensland.org.au/listing/itinerary/creb-track/); [Queensland: CREB Track journey](https://www.queensland.com/au/en/plan-your-holiday/journeys/p-5f3c7ad20b52851034dac82c-creb-track); [Tourism Town: The CREB Track](https://www.tourismtown.com/stores/daintree/tours.95/cape-york-tours.2548/downloads/502/).

### Donovan's Creek

- **Claim:** Roaring Meg Creek and Donovan's Creek are among the crossings, and the streams near the end run crystal clear.
- **Sources:** [Ryan Moody Fishing: Tackling the CREB track (Part 1)](https://www.ryanmoodyfishing.com/creb-track-part-1/); [Dangerous Roads: CREB Track](https://www.dangerousroads.org/australia-and-oceania/australia/8751-creb-track.html).
- **Game choice:** the creeks' exact places on the track aren't documented; their order on the map is a best guess.

### Wujal Wujal

- **Claim:** The Lutheran Church set up a mission on the Bloomfield River in 1886, and the Kuku Nyungkal people still living on their country were moved onto it. In 1979 Kuku Yalanji people began to resettle there and the name changed to Wujal Wujal.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Kuku Nyungkal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuku_Nyungkal); [Queensland Government: Wujal Wujal community history](https://qld.gov.au/firstnations/cultural-awareness-heritage-arts/community-histories/community-histories-u-y/community-histories-wujal-wujal); [Queensland Places: Wujal Wujal](https://queenslandplaces.com.au/node/4984); [Wikipedia: Wujal Wujal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wujal_Wujal).
- **Left out:** the mission's failure within 16 years, and the 1957 attempt to move groups to a Bloomfield mission (both in Wikipedia's Kuku Yalanji entry). True, but more than a short note can carry fairly.
- **Spelling:** Queensland Places writes "Kuku Yalarni"; the game uses the common spelling, Kuku Yalanji.

### Wujal Wujal Falls

- **Claim:** Wujal Wujal is usually translated as "many falls". Upstream of the community the Bloomfield River falls about 40 metres. The falls are a women's place and sacred to the community.
- **Sources:** [About the North: Wujal Wujal](https://www.aboutthenorth.au/styled-126/styled-149/styled-140/styled-157/styled-220/); [About the North: Wujal Wujal Aboriginal Shire](https://www.aboutthenorth.au/styled-6/styled-5/styled-13/styled-14/styled-64/); [Queensland Places: Wujal Wujal Aboriginal Shire Council](https://queenslandplaces.com.au/category/headwords/wujal-wujal-aboriginal-shire-council).
- **Disputed:** Queensland Places glosses the name as "a place with plenty of water". The game says "usually translated", which both readings allow.
- **Game choice:** the note asks the player to look from the track and leave the water alone, and nothing in the game rewards going into the pool.

### The Lions Den Hotel (closing note)

- **Claim:** Built in 1875 at the junction of Mungumby Creek and the Little Annan River for the tin and gold miners of the area; in the same family until 1964. In December 2023 floodwater from the Annan rose over three metres inside; it was rebuilt and fully reopened five months later.
- **Sources:** [Port Douglas Magazine 46: 150 years on, the Lions Den Hotel still roars](https://tourismportdouglas.com.au/port-douglas-magazine-read-online/issue-46/150-years-on-the-lions-den-hotel-still-roars); [Port Douglas Magazine 34, page 88](https://tourismportdouglas.com.au/fileadmin/pdmag/34/files/basic-html/page88.html); [Accomnews: Lion's Den is for sale (2013)](https://www.accomnews.com.au/2013/07/lion-s-den-is-for-sale/); [Wikipedia: Annan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annan_River).
- **Disputed or left out:** the founders' names (no people in the game); the first licence (one source says 1888); Jack Ross's age at death; the stories behind the name.
