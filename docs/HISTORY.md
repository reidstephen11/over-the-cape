# History sources

The history in the game is kept to what these sources say. Where they disagree, the game leaves the disputed detail out (noted under each place). Sources were read in October 2026.

## Episode 1: the CREB Track

### The track (briefing)

- **Claim:** The Cairns Regional Electricity Board cut the CREB as the service track for the power line to Cooktown. The line now runs further inland and the track is used by four-wheel drives (and motorbikes and mountain bikes, not mentioned in the game).
- **Claim:** It opens once the wet season's damage is repaired, and the opening depends on the weather.
- **Sources:** [Cape York Weekly: Unofficial caretaker concerned about the future of CREB Track](https://capeyorkweekly.com.au/unofficial-caretaker-concerned-about-the-future-of-creb-track/7644/); [Douglas Shire Council: The CREB Track](https://douglas.qld.gov.au/the-creb-track/); [Douglas Shire Council: CREB Track opening 22 August 2020](https://douglas.qld.gov.au/creb-track-opening-22-august-2020/); [Newsport: Gearing up for a big Creb Track opening, weather permitting (June 2026)](https://www.newsport.com.au/2026/june/gearing-up-for-a-big-creb-track-opening-weather-permitting); [HEMA Maps: The CREB Track](https://hemamaps.com/blogs/location-guides/the-creb-track-cape-york-qld); [Isuzu UTE: Tropic Wonder](https://www.isuzuute.com.au/discover/featured-stories/tropic-wonder).
- **Left out:** the year the track and line were built. No source found gives it. One source says the track fell out of use when the Bloomfield Track was cut nearer the coast; another says the line moved inland. The game uses only the second.
- **Disputed:** the length (71 km, 77 km and about 50 km are all given). The game doesn't state it.

### The old line camp, the linesman's hut and the poles (side trips)

- **Claim:** none beyond the briefing's: the CREB cut the track to service the power line to Cooktown, and the line has since moved inland (sources above).
- **Game choice:** the two huts, the crates with a jerry can and tyre plugs, and the leaning poles without wires are made up for the game. No source found describes line camps or huts on the CREB, so the notes describe what is in the game, not what was there.
- **Left out:** when the line was built and when its poles came down. A search in October 2026 turned up forum posts on ExplorOz ([CREB Track](https://www.exploroz.com/forum/19143/creb-track), [Comment: CREB Track](https://www.exploroz.com/forum/89082/comment-creb-track)) saying the line was being built in the late 1960s and that most of the poles were pulled out in 2009, after the line was rerouted inland via Lakeland. The pages couldn't be opened to check them, and forum posts aren't a firm source, so the game states neither.

### Daintree Village

- **Claim:** George Elphinstone Dalrymple named the Daintree River in December 1873 after the geologist Richard Daintree, who never came to the region. Dalrymple reported red cedar; cutters followed and floated logs down the river. By 1888 the cedar was nearly gone and the trade had collapsed. The village was the cutters' base.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Daintree River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daintree_River); [Wikipedia: Daintree, Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daintree,_Queensland); [Port Douglas Magazine 44: The history of Daintree Village](https://tourismportdouglas.com.au/port-douglas-magazine/issue-44/the-history-of-daintree-village); [Destination Daintree: Daintree Village](https://www.destinationdaintree.com/daintreevillage.html); [Destination Daintree: historical timeline](https://destinationdaintree.com/the-daintree/history-culture/the-daintree-historical-timeline).
- **Disputed:** when the first settlers arrived (1879 in one timeline, "late 1870s and early 1880s" in Wikipedia). Left out.

### Daintree River crossing

- **Claim:** The crossing is wide and about a metre deep; drivers take a wide arc downstream to stay in the shallowest water. Saltwater crocodiles live in the river, so the crossing shouldn't be walked.
- **Sources:** [ExplorOz: CREB Track](https://www.exploroz.com/routes/creb-track); [Club 4X4: Travel the CREB](https://www.club4x4.com.au/travel-the-creb-bloomfield-track-loop/); [Nick and Pam: Cape York Part One (2018)](https://nickandpam.com/2018/09/11/cape-york-part-one/); [Ryan Moody Fishing: Tackling the CREB track (Part 1)](https://www.ryanmoodyfishing.com/creb-track-part-1/).
- **Game choice:** the shallow line is drawn as a gravel bar curving downstream.

### Big Red and Bob's Hill

- **Claim:** Trip guides name the steep Big Red climb as the most challenging point, with a bypass round it, and Bob's Hill, about halfway, as arguably the hardest part. Both are said "to many drivers" in the game because the guides disagree on which is worst.
- **Sources:** [The Ultimate Guide to Traveling the CREB Track (Dangerous Roads)](https://www.dangerousroads.org/australia-and-oceania/australia/8751-creb-track.html); [True North Adventures: The CREB Track](https://truenorthadventures.com.au/the-creb-track/); [Explore Cape York: The CREB Track](https://explorecapeyork.com.au/journey-to-the-tip-of-australia/creb-track/); [Tropical North Queensland: CREB Track itinerary](https://tropicalnorthqueensland.org.au/listing/itinerary/creb-track/).
- **Game choice:** the red clay going slippery in the wet and tyres being cut on rock are general four-wheel-driving advice, not claims about these hills.

### The range lookout: the 1983–84 blockade and World Heritage

- **Claim:** Douglas Shire Council began the road from Cape Tribulation to Bloomfield in 1983. Protesters blockaded the work in December 1983 and again in 1984. The road was finished, but the protest drew national attention, which led to the nomination of Queensland's wet tropical rainforests for World Heritage listing; they were inscribed in 1988, and commercial logging in them was banned.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Bloomfield Track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomfield_Track); [Wikipedia: Cape Tribulation, Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Tribulation,_Queensland); [Daintree Blockade: The Bloomfield Track](https://www.daintreeblockade.com.au/daintree-blockade-road-today.html); [ExplorOz: Bloomfield Track](https://www.exploroz.com/treks/bloomfield-track).
- **Note:** this is the coastal Bloomfield Track, not the CREB; the game tells it at the lookout because the two run side by side through the same forest.

### The concrete

- **Claim:** The last big descent towards Wujal Wujal is concreted, with cuts across it for traction, and is very steep.
- **Sources:** [Tropical North Queensland: CREB Track itinerary](https://tropicalnorthqueensland.org.au/listing/itinerary/creb-track/); [Queensland: CREB Track journey](https://www.queensland.com/au/en/plan-your-holiday/journeys/p-5f3c7ad20b52851034dac82c-creb-track); [Tourism Town: The CREB Track](https://www.tourismtown.com/stores/daintree/tours.95/cape-york-tours.2548/downloads/502/).

### Donovan's Creek

- **Claim:** Roaring Meg Creek and Donovan's Creek are among the crossings, and the streams near the end run crystal clear.
- **Sources:** [Ryan Moody Fishing: Tackling the CREB track (Part 1)](https://www.ryanmoodyfishing.com/creb-track-part-1/); [Dangerous Roads: CREB Track](https://www.dangerousroads.org/australia-and-oceania/australia/8751-creb-track.html).
- **Game choice:** the creeks' exact places on the track aren't documented; their order on the map is a best guess.

### Wujal Wujal

- **Claim:** The Lutheran Church set up a mission on the Bloomfield River in 1886, and the Kuku Nyungkal people still living on their country were moved onto it. In 1979 Kuku Yalanji people began to resettle there and the name changed to Wujal Wujal.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Kuku Nyungkal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuku_Nyungkal); [Queensland Government: Wujal Wujal community history](https://qld.gov.au/firstnations/cultural-awareness-heritage-arts/community-histories/community-histories-u-y/community-histories-wujal-wujal); [Queensland Places: Wujal Wujal](https://queenslandplaces.com.au/node/4984); [Wikipedia: Wujal Wujal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wujal_Wujal).
- **Left out:** the mission's failure within 16 years, and the 1957 attempt to move groups to a Bloomfield mission (both in Wikipedia's Kuku Yalanji entry). True, but more than a short note can carry fairly.
- **Spelling:** Queensland Places writes "Kuku Yalarni"; the game uses the common spelling, Kuku Yalanji.

### Wujal Wujal Falls

- **Claim:** Wujal Wujal is usually translated as "many falls". Upstream of the community the Bloomfield River falls about 40 metres. The falls are a women's place and sacred to the community.
- **Sources:** [About the North: Wujal Wujal](https://www.aboutthenorth.au/styled-126/styled-149/styled-140/styled-157/styled-220/); [About the North: Wujal Wujal Aboriginal Shire](https://www.aboutthenorth.au/styled-6/styled-5/styled-13/styled-14/styled-64/); [Queensland Places: Wujal Wujal Aboriginal Shire Council](https://queenslandplaces.com.au/category/headwords/wujal-wujal-aboriginal-shire-council).
- **Disputed:** Queensland Places glosses the name as "a place with plenty of water". The game says "usually translated", which both readings allow.
- **Game choice:** the note asks the player to look from the track and leave the water alone, and nothing in the game rewards going into the pool.

### The Lions Den Hotel (closing note)

- **Claim:** Built in 1875 at the junction of Mungumby Creek and the Little Annan River for the tin and gold miners of the area; in the same family until 1964. In December 2023 floodwater from the Annan rose over three metres inside; it was rebuilt and fully reopened five months later.
- **Sources:** [Port Douglas Magazine 46: 150 years on, the Lions Den Hotel still roars](https://tourismportdouglas.com.au/port-douglas-magazine-read-online/issue-46/150-years-on-the-lions-den-hotel-still-roars); [Port Douglas Magazine 34, page 88](https://tourismportdouglas.com.au/fileadmin/pdmag/34/files/basic-html/page88.html); [Accomnews: Lion's Den is for sale (2013)](https://www.accomnews.com.au/2013/07/lion-s-den-is-for-sale/); [Wikipedia: Annan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annan_River).
- **Disputed or left out:** the founders' names (no people in the game); the first licence (one source says 1888); Jack Ross's age at death; the stories behind the name.

## Episode 2: the Old Coach Road

### The road (briefing)

- **Claim:** Gold was found on the Palmer River in 1873 and a rush followed. Maytown became the centre of the field. The road between Maytown and Laura was built from about 1877 to about 1895, over the Conglomerate Range; Maytown had no road directly to it until 1877. Cobb & Co began regular coach services on it in 1880.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Laura to Maytown Coach Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_to_Maytown_Coach_Road); [State Library of Queensland: Queensland Places, Laura–Maytown Coach Road](https://www.slq.qld.gov.au/blog/queensland-places-laura-maytown-coach-road); [Wikipedia: Maytown, Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maytown,_Queensland).
- **Disputed:** one travel account says Cobb & Co ran coaches "to Maytown and the goldfields" generally; the game uses only the State Library's 1880 start on this road.
- **Game choice:** the episode runs south to north, from Maytown to Laura, as the other episodes do; most people drive it the other way today. The dry-season closing (December to March) and the road's toughness (low range most of the way) come from trip reports ([Outback Travel Australia](https://outbacktravelaustralia.com.au/destinations-travel-destinations/laura-to-maytown-coach-road-january-2016/); [Grey Gypsies: Old Coach Road](https://greygypsies.com.au/cape_york/old_coach_road.htm); [4x4 Earth: Laura to Maytown](https://tracks.4x4earth.com/4wd-track/laura-to-maytown/619)).

### The Palmer River (start note)

- **Claim:** A prospecting party led by James Venture Mulligan found payable gold on the Palmer River in 1873, and the rush followed. A track was blazed to the field from Cooktown, reaching Palmerville in November 1873.
- **Sources:** [Queensland Parks: Palmer Goldfield, nature, culture and history](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/palmer-goldfield/about/culture); [Queensland Heritage Register: Maytown](https://apps.des.qld.gov.au/heritage-register/explorer/detail/?id=602255); [Wikipedia: Maytown, Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maytown,_Queensland).
- **Disputed:** the date of the find (29 June 1873 in the parks history, August 1873 in Wikipedia) and the credit for it (the Heritage Register says William Hann first noticed gold in August 1872 and Mulligan's party confirmed payable gold). The note gives only the year and says Mulligan's party found payable gold, which all three allow.
- **Left out:** numbers of diggers, which no source checked gives.

### Maytown

- **Claim:** It began as a camp late in 1873 and was first called Edwardstown. In May 1875 it became the field's administrative and business centre, and it came to have one of the largest Chinese populations in Australia. The school closed in 1925 and the town was largely abandoned by 1945. What remains includes the baker's oven, the stone kerbing and channelling along Leslie Street, a cemetery, and a replica hut in Duff Street built by the Palmer River Historical Preservation Society.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Maytown, Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maytown,_Queensland); [Queensland Heritage Register: Maytown](https://apps.des.qld.gov.au/heritage-register/explorer/detail/?id=602255).
- **Disputed:** when the name Maytown came in (by May 1878 in one account; in use by 1874 in another, which also disputes the story that it was named for a surveyor's daughter). The note leaves out when and why.
- **Game choice:** the ruins are drawn where they fit the map; their layout is not copied from the site.

### The North Palmer River

- **Claim:** Before it crosses the North Palmer River the road divides in two, and both branches are kept; one served as the wet-season crossing.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Laura to Maytown Coach Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_to_Maytown_Coach_Road) (from the Heritage Register listing).
- **Game choice:** one line goes straight through the sand and the other crosses a rocky bar upstream; the sources don't describe the two crossings' beds.

### The Conglomerate Range

- **Claim:** Early works reduced the road's gradients and added retaining walls and drainage; heavy rain and wheeled traffic cut into the soft sandstone, above all on the descent from the Conglomerate Range.
- **Sources:** [State Library of Queensland: Queensland Places, Laura–Maytown Coach Road](https://www.slq.qld.gov.au/blog/queensland-places-laura-maytown-coach-road).
- **Game choice:** the three ways up the range's face come from a trip report of one steep pinch with three lines: the overgrown line the coaches used, the steepest line in the middle, and an easier one to the side ([Grey Gypsies: Old Coach Road](https://greygypsies.com.au/cape_york/old_coach_road.htm)). Where on the road that pinch is isn't documented; the game puts it on the range's south face. Likewise the big rock step with a track round it to the east is from a forum report ([ExplorOz forum: Laura to Maytown on Old Coach Road](https://www.exploroz.com/forum/102566/laura-to-maytown-on-old-coach-road---alone-with-a-trailer)); its place on the range is a best guess.

### Hotel site

- **Claim:** Remnants along the road include the sites of Chalmer's, Patrick's and Ned's hotels, gold workings and batteries, staging posts, and Chinese market gardens.
- **Sources:** [State Library of Queensland: Queensland Places, Laura–Maytown Coach Road](https://www.slq.qld.gov.au/blog/queensland-places-laura-maytown-coach-road).
- **Left out:** which hotel stood where. The camp on the range is called only "Hotel site". A forum post names a Folders Hotel site and a cairn on the road; neither could be checked, so neither is in the game.

### Laura (closing note)

- **Claim:** The Laura to Maytown Coach Road was entered on the Queensland Heritage Register on 21 October 1992, its cuttings and drainage listed as its significant parts; stretches keep their original construction. The sandstone escarpments round Laura hold a large body of Aboriginal rock art characterised by Quinkans, spirit figures; Quinkan Country was entered on the National Heritage List on 10 November 2018.
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Laura to Maytown Coach Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_to_Maytown_Coach_Road); [Wikipedia: Quinkan rock art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinkan_rock_art); [Ninti One: Aboriginal rock art of the Laura valleys](https://www.nintione.com.au/resources/nol/aboriginal-rock-art-of-the-laura-valleys-one-landscape-many-stories/).
- **Left out:** the Cooktown–Laura railway, which wasn't checked against a source.

## Episode 3: Lakefield

The sources for this episode were found by web search in October 2026. The pages themselves couldn't be opened from the machine the game was built on, so each claim below rests on the passages of them that the searches quoted. Where those passages disagreed, the game leaves the detail out or words it so that both allow it.

### The diary and the stockman (briefing, the bog, closing note)

- **Game choice:** the diary entry, its date (Friday 28 November 1986), the man at the store, the stockman and his bogged Land Cruiser are all made up, as SPEC.md §2a allows. Nothing in the game says a real stockman was bogged there.
- **Claim used in the diary:** the park shuts for the wet. Today it is closed every wet season, from 1 December, because heavy rain makes its roads impassable; the reopening depends on the season ([Queensland Parks: Wet season closure, Rinyirru (Lakefield) National Park](https://parks.des.qld.gov.au/park-alerts/23476); [Queensland Parks: Rinyirru wet season closure (PDF)](https://parks.desi.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0023/163823/rinyirru-wet-closure.pdf)). The diary says only that it will shut "any day now", not that the 1986 dates were the same.

### The park (briefing)

- **Claim:** Lakefield National Park was gazetted in 1979 (on 25 October) over what had been two cattle stations, Laura and Lakefield, used for grazing for about a hundred years before. The Queensland Government bought the two stations in 1978.
- **Sources:** [Queensland Parks: Rinyirru (Lakefield) Aggregation Management Statement 2013 (PDF)](https://parks.des.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0027/165771/rinyirru.pdf); [Queensland Parks: Rinyirru (Lakefield) National Park, nature, culture and history](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/rinyirru-lakefield/about/culture); [Wikipedia: Rinyirru (Lakefield) National Park](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rinyirru_(Lakefield)_National_Park>).
- **Disputed:** one secondary site gives 1979 for the purchase. The game uses the parks service's 1978.

### Old Laura homestead

- **Claim:** Peter McDermott and Fergus O'Beirne made the earliest application for a run on the lower Laura River in October 1879. The main house is dated to 1902, a date that rests on a single narrative history. The station was sold in 1966 (the Lakefield Cattle Company bought Laura and Lakefield and moved to a new homestead about 24 km north), and the old homestead was left. The Queensland Government bought it in 1978, and restoration began in 1986.
- **Sources:** [Queensland Heritage Register: Old Laura Homestead](https://apps.des.qld.gov.au/heritage-register/detail/?id=602210); [Wikipedia: Old Laura Homestead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Laura_Homestead); the management statement above.
- **Game choice:** the note says the house "is thought to date from about 1902" because the Register flags that date as thin. The buildings are drawn as two plain huts and a tank, not copied from the site.
- **Left out:** that the station supplied beef to the Palmer goldfield (one secondary source only).

### Lakefield

- **Claim:** Lakefield was taken up as a grazing property around 1878. The old homestead, a timber and corrugated iron building, was the hub of the Lakefield cattle station in the early 1900s, and was abandoned in the early 1960s when a new homestead was built.
- **Sources:** the management statement above; [Queensland Parks: Historic Lakefield Homestead day-use area](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/rinyirru-lakefield/attractions/historic-lakefield-homestead-day-use-area).

### The black soil plains

- **Claim:** none about the place's history. That black soil sets hard in the dry and turns to a bogging grease when wet is general four-wheel-driving advice, and the wet-season closure notices above say the rain makes the park's roads impassable.

### Hann Crossing

- **Claim:** Hann Crossing is on the North Kennedy River (the Hann River joins it downstream), and is reached by four-wheel drive.
- **Sources:** [Queensland Parks: Hann Crossing camping area](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/rinyirru-lakefield/camping/hann-crossing); [Wikipedia: Hann River (Queensland)](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hann_River_(Queensland)>).
- **Game choice:** no source found describes the crossing's bed, so the bar across the river and its depth are made up for the game, as is how fast the river rises in the storm.
- **Left out:** that the crossing is named after the explorer William Hann. Only a travel guide says so.

### Musgrave (closing note)

- **Claim:** Musgrave was one of the stations on the Cape York telegraph line, which ran from Laura to Thursday Island and was finished in 1887. It was named after Sir Anthony Musgrave, governor of Queensland from 1883 to 1888. The telegraph office closed on 22 June 1929 and the building was used afterwards as a cattle station's homestead. It is now a roadhouse, and was entered on the Queensland Heritage Register in 1992.
- **Sources:** [State Library of Queensland: Queensland Places, Musgrave Telegraph Station](https://www.slq.qld.gov.au/blog/queensland-places-musgrave-telegraph-station); [Wikipedia: Musgrave Telegraph Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musgrave_Telegraph_Station); [Queensland Heritage Register: Musgrave Telegraph Station (former)](https://apps.des.qld.gov.au/heritage-register/explorer/detail/?id=600431); [Wikipedia: Cape York Telegraph Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_York_Telegraph_Line).
- **Disputed:** when the station was built (1886 in the heritage sources, with the office opening on 23 December 1886; 1887 on roadhouse and tour pages). The note says "in the 1880s".
- **Left out:** when the telegraph line itself closed (SPEC.md §2a: to be checked before the game states it).

### Rinyirru (closing note)

- **Claim:** the park was handed back to its Traditional Owners in 2011 and renamed Rinyirru (Lakefield) National Park; it is managed jointly by the Rinyirru (Lakefield) Aboriginal Corporation and the Queensland Government. Elders had objected that the 1979 declaration was made without consulting them.
- **Sources:** [Australian Geographic: Biggest park in Cape York handed back (June 2011)](https://www.australiangeographic.com.au/news/2011/06/biggest-park-in-cape-york-handed-back/); [Queensland Parks: Historic Lakefield Homestead day-use area](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/rinyirru-lakefield/attractions/historic-lakefield-homestead-day-use-area).
- **Left out:** the objection to the 1979 declaration. True, but more than a closing line can carry fairly.
