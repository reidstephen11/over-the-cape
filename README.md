# Over the Cape

A single-player 4WD adventure up Cape York, told in episodes, that runs in the browser and can be installed from Chrome on Android. Each episode is a stage of the trip north, from the Daintree to the tip of Australia, with its own map, a core mission, the weather and time of day the story sets, and a starting load of fuel, spare wheels and tyre plugs, which side trips off the main line can add to. Run out of fuel, get stranded with a flat and nothing to fix it with, wreck the engine or miss the deadline, and the episode ends; you restart from the last camp you reached. History notes at the places you pass tell what happened there.

The story follows a grandparent's 1986 trip up the Cape through their diary, and the vehicles are being rebuilt with more accurate body shapes: see [SPEC.md](SPEC.md) for the game's direction.

It is built on the code of [4WD Simulator](https://github.com/reidstephen11/4wd-simulator) and keeps its look and driving: Three.js, Rapier physics and TypeScript, with its terrain, trees, leaf textures, vehicles and sound generated in code. The title screen uses the approved camp-at-dusk artwork as its backdrop. Reference photographs for the vehicles live in [`reference/vehicles/`](reference/vehicles/README.md) and are not loaded by the game.

## Play

**Online:** https://reidstephen11.github.io/over-the-cape/ (deployed from `main` by GitHub Actions). On Android Chrome, Install app / Add to Home screen opens it as a standalone app (v0.82). A new version downloads in the background; it takes over on the title screen, and during a drive it waits for **Pause → Restart for the new version** (v1.23), so a deploy never reloads the page mid-drive. The service worker keeps every file, including the physics engine's WebAssembly (a file of its own since v1.46), so after one visit the game also starts offline.

**Locally** (Node 20 or newer):

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # then open http://localhost:5173
```

**On Batocera** (x86-64): `bash batocera/deploy.sh [host]` builds the game and installs it as a Ports entry over SSH (default host `batocera-nuc.local`). Run it again after an update; saves are kept. Use the Batocera exit hotkey to quit.

| Script              | What it does                                                                     |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `npm run build`     | Type-checks and builds the static site into `dist/`                              |
| `npm run preview`   | Serves `dist/` at http://localhost:4173                                          |
| `npm run lint`      | ESLint and Prettier check                                                        |
| `npm test`          | Unit and headless physics tests (Vitest); `npm test -- --shard=1/3` runs a third |
| `npm run typecheck` | Type-check everything, tests and scripts included (the build checks only `src`)  |
| `npm run test:e2e`  | Browser tests (Playwright; builds first)                                         |

The game opens at a riverside camp at dusk. **Press any button** (or **Tap to begin**) brings up **Continue**, **New Journey** and **Settings**, with quiet camp ambience. **Continue** loads your saved camp and starts driving without another briefing confirmation. **New Journey** opens episode selection; **Vehicle & difficulty** also contains replay conditions for finished episodes. Choosing a new journey does not delete the saved camp while you browse. **Settings** offers graphics, master volume, controller type, phone driving, speed units, HUD style and, while a controller is connected on a computer, the gearbox (Automatic or Manual, v1.35), preserving your progress.

Choose **Controller type** in the title or pause **Settings**: **PlayStation (PS4)**, **Xbox** or **Generic** (numbered buttons). The choice is saved; existing saves keep PlayStation labels. On a phone, **Phone driving** chooses **Joystick only** (the default) or **Steer and pedals**. Driving, recovery, camera, map and controls-help prompts use that choice when a controller is active. Using the keyboard brings key labels back automatically; using touch brings back the phone controls. The title and pause navigation hints also follow the current input.

Use the arrow keys and Enter, the controller's d-pad and its confirm button, or touch. Escape or the controller's back button goes back. The background drifts slowly; reduced-motion preferences disable this and the prompt animation. The title loads before the driving engine, which is downloaded only when you choose to play. Sound starts with the first gesture, respects the volume setting and fades into driving.

For a new run, the loading **briefing** shows the mission, weather and deadline. **Story & trip details** and **Controls** expand when wanted; press **Drive** when ready. **Pause menu → Main menu** returns to the title with progress kept.

The version number (for example v0.01) is on the loading screen and the pause menu, and goes up by 0.01 with every update merged into `main`. Hover over it to see the exact build.

## The CREB Track

The game is one episode, the CREB Track, made hard. Episodes 2–4 (the Old Coach Road, Lakefield and the Frenchmans Track) were removed in v0.73. A finished run can be replayed, in the story's conditions or in your choice of time of day and weather.

**Mission: reach the Lions Den Hotel before dark.** Watch the engine temperature gauge: working it hard overheats it, fastest when bogged with the wheels spinning; airflow and water cool it, and once it's hot it loses power and starts to get damaged. Let the tyres down (tools menu, while stopped) for more grip on clay and rock and fewer punctures from sharp stones, and pump them up again before the faster gravel, where a soft tyre can roll off its rim in a hard corner; the compressor takes a minute, about 4½ minutes on the clock. Wet clay, mud, track dirt and grass cut up where the wheels spin (wet grass fastest of all, so the verge is no easy way round), and the churned ground shows as dark wet patches, so each failed attempt at a climb makes the next one slipperier: back off before you dig it in. Two drivers are bogged beside the track, a Patrol on the ridge and a LandCruiser towards Donovan's Creek: you don't have to stop, but winch them out and they'll give you a jerry can or tyre plugs. Take deep crossings slowly: above about 9 km/h the bow wave climbs the bonnet to the air intake, and the faster you go the quicker it floods the engine. Around 15 km/h through a creek the engine sputters; at 30 km/h it stalls in the water. On the narrow CREB Track you'll meet other 4WDs coming the other way: they stop for you, so pull off to one side and let them past. If dark catches you, carry on by headlights: the run only ends at 8 pm, but a finish after dark scores nothing for time. It is June, soon after the track reopens, and every run rolls how you find it: **dried out hard** (the clay holds, the bogs have shrunk back, the creeks are down), **just reopened** after overnight rain (greasy clay, soft bogs), or **wet**, with the range still letting go (slick clay, deep bogs, every creek running). The briefing says which. The afternoon storm comes in either way, harder on a wet day than a dry one, and restarting from a camp finds the track as you left it. You leave Daintree Village at 12:30 pm; it's dark at 6:15 pm. Storms are forecast from 3 pm. On board: 50 L of fuel, one 20 L jerry can, one spare wheel and three tyre plugs.

The map is a long strip, 1.3 km wide and 6 km from south to north, with about 6.6 km of track up its length. The real trip is over 100 km, so the distances are shortened; the obstacles are full size.

**The paper map fills in as you drive** (v1.04). It starts as blank paper with only your camp drawn; the ground within about 150 m of where the car has been appears as you go, tracks, creeks and contours included. A pencilled **?** marks a place you haven't found yet within about 250 m of ground you've seen. What you've drawn is kept at each camp: a restart from camp goes back to the map as it was there, and a new run starts with a blank sheet. Test mode (`?dev`) shows the whole map.

1. **Daintree Village** (camp) down to the **Daintree River crossing**. The road out of the village is an orientation over a spur of the range: signs take the controls one at a time. Low range (L) on the ramp up onto a bench, precision crawl (P) over a band of rock on it, the diff locks (Z, then X) on the pinch to the top, then the winch (E to hook, R and F to wind) at a bog in the hollow before the river. The crossing is wide and about 1.2 m deep in the middle, running fast enough to carry a car off the straight line. A gravel bar downstream keeps it about half that, but after the wet the bar is strewn with boulders and scoured into holes over a metre deep, right across the straight line over it: weave between them. The river is running fast after the rain: the current pushes the car downstream, and in the deep water it can carry it away. Crocodile country: you never leave the car.
   - **The knoll play area** (v1.47), east of the village road before the river, is a place to warm up and explore. A higher top joins the knoll on its east side, and the **Knoll track** loops round it from the village road (signposted "Knoll play area" just out of the village) to the river crossing. Signed lines leave the loop: the **rock climb** straight up the south face over split granite ledges near half a metre high on a pitch near 28° (hard: low range, both lockers, crawl); the **switchbacks**, four legs up the east face with tight hairpins; the **side slope**, round the west flank leaning you out more than 20°, then down to the loop by the river; the **drop**, down the north-west face and off a rock lip over a near-vertical bank about 3 m high into a mud hole (hard, one way down); the **steep descent**, straight down the north face past 30° through erosion ruts; the **axle twisters**, holes and mounds under alternate wheels; and the **bog trench**, a long bog sunk between clay walls higher than the bonnet, with trees to winch to. Each line is signed at both ends; the far sign says whether it is one way (the rock climb is up only, the drop and the steep descent down only) or can be driven either way. None of it is on the route.
2. The **paddock road** to the foot of the range is still pocked with potholes (signposted): at about 30 km/h they only rattle the car, but much faster they pinch the tyres. Every few hundred metres there is a bog in a hollow or a creek coming off the range. The bogs are wider than the road, so they cannot be missed; the creeks have cut their beds below the paddocks, so the road drops into the water on the centre line and climbs the wet bank out (v0.88: the north creek is no longer a hidden wall under the water). That north creek’s entry and exit are deep sticky mud right across the track: low range, both lockers and keep moving; a marked rock on the far bank is there if you bog.
3. **Big Red**: about 175 m of red clay worn into the face of the spur, greasy after the rain. It wanders up the hillside rather than running straight, in two pinches of about 20–23° with an easier stretch between them, and a rut under the left wheel. Low range, both lockers and precision crawl, kept on the crown, creep up it (the Camel Trophy Ninety included, in the dry, without boiling). Drift left onto the bank and it grounds. High range won't get up it, nor low range with the diffs open. Marked winch rocks stand just right of the track, ahead of each pinch: if you stall, USE hooks the one ahead and IN pulls you up the climb. The **west line** leaves the paddock road just before. It is longer, and it is not the easy way: a bog, then pinches about as steep as the face up the spur's west flank, with a rock step, and over its shoulder.
4. Along the range through the rainforest (a signposted spur east leads to the **Old line camp**, with two tyre plugs), down into a saddle with a **deep bog** in it. The **chicken track** to the west is off-camber and then climbs about as steep as the bog line, with a rock step: it avoids the bog and costs the time. Then a steep clay pinch that also needs low range and the lockers, and a **washout** cut right across the track (signposted), which needs the planks in every vehicle but the Unimog, to **Roaring Meg camp**, then the fast ford over **Roaring Meg Creek** and up its slippery bank, with a short track to **Roaring Meg Falls**. The falls are just downstream of the ford. Above the gorge the track climbs a **rock ledge** about a metre high: crawl up it in low range with both lockers.
5. **Bob's Hill**: a steep, rutted pinch at about 21° up the knob. Like Big Red it needs low range and both lockers (the Unimog gets up with its diffs open).
6. Along the range top, round a **sideling** that leans the car over towards the drop, and where a track signposted **Old line** follows a few leaning poles down the west flank to the **Linesman's hut** and a full jerry can; then the **range lookout**, then **the concrete**: a long switchback descent with concreted pinches at about 20°. Where it swings west, the **steep shortcut** runs straight down the spur over rock ledges: quicker, but crawl it in low range or the rock cuts your tyres.
7. **Donovan's Creek**, the **Bloomfield River** timber bridge at Wujal Wujal (camp), and a track west to **Wujal Wujal Falls**. The wet has pulled boards out of the deck: crawl it, keep your wheels on the timber you can see (a hole on the right, then the left, then a gap down the middle you straddle). Off the boards is a drop into the river; restart from the camp if you go in. The river is too deep to ford.
8. North on the **Bloomfield Track**. A bog, a creek or a climb comes every few hundred metres, through **Rossville** and across the **Little Annan River**, to the **Lions Den Hotel**. The road goes straight over two knolls: on the **Rossville pinch** the gravel has washed off, leaving wet red clay at about 21°, and on the **granite pinch** near Helenvale ledges of rock stand out of the climb. Both need low range and both lockers: open diffs stop on them. Three of the creeks come off the hills (one with a scour hole just below the ford): keep moving in the water. The road fords the Little Annan where it is deepest; the longer **shallow crossing** goes downstream over a gravel bar at about half the depth.

After the wet the red clay tracks are slick, the gravel roads are bumpy and potholed, and the range tracks are washed out, with channels cut across them. The potholes and washouts deep enough to pinch a tyre at speed are dark, ringed with pale gravel. Water runs off the crown of the road and lies along its edges, so the darker strip down each side, and the bare red clay banks beside the cuttings, are mud: keep your wheels off them. Otherwise each stretch has its own kind of trouble (above) rather than mud all the way. The saddle on the ridge still holds a deep bog full of brown water, and one long wet stretch lies below Roaring Meg camp. The other deep bogs are dug across the road: before the river, twice on the paddock road, on the west line, and twice on the Bloomfield Track. They are wider than the shoulders, so the line cannot slip round them. A marked boulder stands ahead of each one to winch from. Where a hole sits in a hollow the land drains into, it is a **deep bog**, about 0.7 m of sticky black mud: few vehicles get out without the winch. Off the tracks most of the flatter ground is wet **black soil**, dark beside the firm verge: drive onto it and you crawl at walking pace, and on any slope you stop. Drive fast and the car bounces off the swells and slides on the clay. The creeks run fast and deep: Donovan's Creek and the Little Annan push hard, and the Daintree's deep middle stops a car dead.

**The afternoon storm.** Showers come in at 3 pm (a note on screen tells you), build to heavy rain by 5 pm and ease at dusk. The sky darkens, the rain drums on the roof, and the clay gets greasier the longer it rains. The creeks rise and run faster: muddier, deeper and pushing harder. At 5 pm the Little Annan is about 15 cm up and anything gets across in low range; by 5:45 it is over 20 cm up, and the short-wheelbase 4WD crawling across is swept away, while driving through at 15 km/h throws a bow wave into the intake that floods the engine and can stall it: take the shallow crossing downstream, or get there earlier. The little creeks across the Bloomfield Track rise less. Each finish is scored out of 1000 for time to spare, the car's condition (engine, suspension and tyres) and the fuel and kit left, with a gold, silver or bronze medal; your best score and time are kept. Big Red, Bob's Hill and the concrete are timed end to end, with a best kept for each; leave the track (the steep shortcut, the Big Red bypass) and the attempt is dropped. Your quickest finish drives along with you on later runs as a pale ghost car, and the pause menu says how far behind or ahead of it you are.

Restarting from a camp brings back the weather and the creeks as they were when you got there. Replays in conditions you choose keep the weather you picked.

The map is rainforest (canopy trees on buttress roots, fan palms and tree ferns) except for open eucalypt woodland round the village, the paddocks and the drier hills by the Lions Den. A fallen tree lies across the track on the range: winch it aside or squeeze past.

**Surprises.** The route isn't the same every run. After the wet, dead trees come down and the clay cuttings shed rocks: each run, two dead trees fall across the track somewhere along the way (in the timber at the top of the paddocks, through the rainforest on the range or on the Bloomfield Track), and rocks come off a cutting's bank onto the track somewhere on the range. You see the dead tree standing at the roadside, then as you come along it cracks and crashes down across the track 45–70 m ahead (further ahead the faster you're going, so there's room to stop): never onto the car. Hook its crown end and winch it round so it lies along one side, then squeeze past on the other. Rocks land on the bank's side of the track, two big ones and a scatter of small ones you can crawl over in low range: keep to the far side, or winch a big one aside. Where they happen changes with each new run; restarting from a camp brings the same ones back in the same places.

Beyond the map's edge the real country carries on to the horizon, brought in closer and lower in step with the map. East, between the range and the Coral Sea, stand Thornton Peak, Mt Pieter Botte, Mt Sorrow behind Cape Tribulation, and Mt Finnigan's granite tops, and the Daintree and the Bloomfield run out to the sea. West, rainforest ranges rise to the flat-topped tablelands of the Great Dividing Range. North of the Lions Den the country dries out to open woodland round the black boulder heaps of Black Mountain. South are the Daintree's cane and cattle flats and the ranges towards Mossman. Far land fades into the sky with distance, as it does on a humid day.

## How an episode works

- **The mission and the clock** sit in the top left: what to do, the time of day and the deadline. The clock runs while you drive (four and a half game minutes a real minute) and stops while paused. The sun moves with it, through late afternoon to dusk, when the headlights come on by themselves.
- **Camps save the run.** Reaching a camp saves where you are, the clock, what's on board and any test-mode trailer, its load and loose drums. Pause menu → **Restart from last camp** goes back to it as things were then. A camp also mends the engine and suspension, cools a boiling engine and patches punctures; it doesn't hand out fuel, spares or plugs. It keeps what you'd found by then: restart from it and anything picked up after it is back where it was, places found after it are new again, section times from the abandoned stretch are dropped, and any car coming the other way is gone (the track sends one again). A tree or rocks that came down before the camp stay down; those still to come happen again the same way.
- **History notes** appear in the top right as you reach a place. Click one to close it.
- **The episode ends early** when, with the difficulty that allows it:
  - the tank is empty, you have no jerry cans left and the car has rolled to a stop (any difficulty);
  - the deadline passes (any difficulty);
  - the current lifts the car and carries it downstream (Mild and Realistic);
  - a tyre is flat and there's nothing left to fix it: no spare, and no plug that fits (Realistic);
  - the engine is wrecked (Realistic).

  The "Episode over" card says why and offers **Restart from camp** or **Main menu**.

- **Reaching the goal** shows the closing history and how the run went (time driven, arrival time, fuel and spares left), with **Main menu** and **Keep driving**.

## Fuel, spares and plugs

- **Fuel** uses each vehicle's real tank capacity, separately from how much the episode puts in it. Most vehicles start with 50 L; the Troop Carrier starts with 110 L in its two 90 L tanks. Consumption follows engine work, with an idle allowance for each engine: climbing, accelerating and wheelspin use more than gentle driving. The episode speeds up the burn to suit the shortened trip (6 times on the CREB). On a clean autopilot run of the CREB in the storm, the short-wheelbase 4WD uses about 30 L and the Unimog about 40 L, so both finish on the 50 L in the tank. The jerry can is for a recovery. The gauge reads litres against the vehicle's full capacity, even when it starts partly filled. Tank sources, consumption checks and their limits are in [docs/FUEL.md](docs/FUEL.md).
- **Jerry cans** hold 20 L. Tool menu → **Pour in a jerry can**, stopped, about 12 s. If the engine ran dry it restarts on the starter afterwards.
- **Tyres** can be hurt two ways with Realistic damage: a **puncture** leaks down over about 40 s and takes a **plug** from the kit (tool menu → Plug the tyre, about 10 s); a **torn sidewall** goes flat in a few seconds and needs a **spare** (tool menu → Fit a spare, about 8 s). A landing of about 1.3 m onto rock punctures a tyre; about 1.9 m tears the sidewall. Spinning a wheel hard on rock for several seconds wears through the tread. Crawling in low range barely spins. Dropping into a pothole or washout at speed pinches the tyre (a puncture): a deep one from about 40 km/h, and the faster the likelier; from about 55 km/h it can bend that corner's suspension too. A **sharp stone** can puncture a tyre at any pace, by chance (v1.07): most often on rock, now and then on gravel, rarely on dirt or clay, never on mud, sand, grass or the concrete strips, and more often the faster you go; about one run in three picks one up. **Let-down tyres** take a harder knock before they're cut and pick up about a third as many sharp stones, but in a hard corner (about 30 km/h on a tight bend) the outside tyre can **roll off its rim**: it's flat at once and needs a spare. A tyre going down squashes flat at the bottom; flat, its corner sits low, it thumps once a turn at speed, it grips poorly in corners and drags, and a flat front tyre pulls the car hard towards its side.
- **Finds off the main line.** Two side tracks on the range lead to huts with something to load: two tyre plugs at the Old line camp (a short level spur off the ridge, signposted "Line camp") and a full 20 L jerry can at the Linesman's hut (about 110 m down the range top's west flank along the old poles, signposted "Old line", with a greasy climb back up). Drive up to the crate by the hut and stop: it goes on board and the counts go up. Each can be taken once. The trip costs daylight and a little fuel, a few minutes there and back; neither is needed on a clean run, but either can save a run that's gone wrong. Three more have no sign or hut (v1.05): other travellers left a spare wheel at the end of the Roaring Meg Falls track, a full jerry can at the back of the range lookout's flat, and two tyre plugs at the end of the Wujal Wujal Falls track. The paper map shows a **?** near anything still to be loaded once you've seen the ground close by.
- Every vehicle starts an episode with **two spare wheels**, unless you pack otherwise.
- **Packing** (v1.01). **New Journey → Packing** sets what a new run takes: fuel in the tank (10 L steps up to a full tank), up to 4 jerry cans, up to 3 spare wheels and up to 6 tyre plugs, with **Standard load** to go back to the episode's own. Each vehicle has a weight limit (a bar shows the load against it) and remembers its own packing. Weight counts: a full jerry can is 22 kg, a litre of fuel 0.8 kg and a spare wheel 30–60 kg (100 kg on the Unimog). A heavier car squats on its springs, sinks deeper in mud, climbs slower and works the engine harder, so it burns more fuel; pack light and you have less to fall back on. Fuel burnt and cans poured in lighten the car as you go. The finish scores the fuel and kit you still have out of what you packed. A restart from a camp keeps the run's load; Continue ignores the packing list.
- The status strip shows the planks on board ("Planks 2/2"); the supplies panel counts jerry cans, spares and plugs.

## Difficulty

**New Journey → Vehicle & difficulty** is the damage setting (also in pause menu → Settings → Damage). It decides what can hurt the car, and so which ways an episode can end.

| Setting   | Damage                                                                                     | Ends the episode                                                       |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Off       | None (the body still dents and gets dirty)                                                 | Empty tank, deadline                                                   |
| Mild      | Tyres go soft, corners bend, the engine loses power; tool menu → Field repair fixes it all | Empty tank, deadline, swept away                                       |
| Realistic | Punctures and torn sidewalls, bent corners, the engine can be wrecked                      | Empty tank, deadline, swept away, a flat with no fix, a wrecked engine |

## Vehicles

Pick one under **New Journey → Vehicle & difficulty**. The short-wheelbase 4WD (40 Series) and the Camel Trophy Land Rover are open from the start; each of the others unlocks with a challenge (v1.06): Hilux, reach the Lions Den; GQ Patrol, winch out the bogged Patrol on the ridge; LC79, winch out the bogged LandCruiser at Donovan's Creek; Troop Carrier, load all five finds in one run; Unimog, reach the Lions Den without using the winch; 80 Series, beat 2:00 up Big Red, 2:00 up Bob's Hill and 3:00 down the concrete; 60 Series, finish the CREB in the 40 Series; 40 Series ute, finish the CREB in the Hilux; Perentie, the **Army supply run** (a Start option: tow two fuel drums to the camp at Wujal Wujal). The vehicle lists show what opens each locked one. Test mode (`?dev`) opens them all. Pause menu → Vehicle changes it mid-episode, but the run restarts from your last camp in the new one. Each episode sets what you start with, and the Troop Carrier carries more on top: 60 L of extra fuel in its two 90 L tanks. These come only at the start of an episode: changing vehicle at a camp carries what's on board across as it is, and fuel that won't fit the new vehicle's tank is left behind.

The 1986 Camel Trophy started at Cooktown, and Army Perenties served across the north. The Camel Trophy car is the event's own vehicle, a yellow Land Rover Ninety: short (2.36 m wheelbase), light, on coil springs and narrow tyres, with a slow 2.5 L diesel and a five-speed gearbox, and the steering wheel on the left. It breathes through its snorkel, so it wades much deeper water before it floods; the current can still take it. The heavier Land Rover holds its ground in a current a little longer. The red Hilux, the first Hilux with 4WD, is light and thrifty: its small petrol four uses about a third less fuel than the six, but it is slower and needs low range sooner, and its long wheelbase and tub hang out further behind the rear wheels. It carries its recovery boards on a ladder rack over the tub. The Army Unimog is the slow, heavy one: 6.4 t on 1.1 m tyres and portal axles, with the most ground clearance by far, a crawling low range, lockers that engage on the move up to 15 km/h, and a winch that pulls three times as hard as the Land Rover's. It tops out at about 68 km/h and takes much longer to get there, and it is thirsty. On a side slope it tips at about the Land Rover's angle. It wades water that would carry the others away, and breathes through a stack up the right screen pillar. Its pintle hook is ready for trailers, which come in a later update. The white Troop Carrier, a common sight on Cape York trips, is the long-range choice: on the CREB it starts with 110 L of diesel. It is the heaviest and tallest of the 4WDs (only the Unimog truck is bigger): slower to get going, it turns a wider circle, it rolls over in a full-lock turn at 50 km/h (the other 4WDs hold on to 60) and on a slightly gentler side slope than the others, and its long body hangs well out behind the rear wheels. Its weight also holds it on the riverbed: water that carries the smaller vehicles away only pushes it a few metres, and it takes deeper, faster water (about 1.5 m running at 2.5 m/s) to carry it off. It still floods once the water reaches its air intake, like the Land Rover's. Its second spare, a jerry can and the recovery boards ride on the roof rack. The GQ Patrol wagon, the other classic Cape tourer, came across from the 4WD Simulator: white over charcoal, with a bull bar carrying the winch and two driving lamps, a snorkel up the windscreen pillar and a full-length roof rack. At about 2.4 t it sits between the Land Rover and the Troop Carrier, and it has the longest wheelbase of the 4WDs (2.97 m), so mind its belly on crests. Its TD42 diesel six is slow and steady, with a knock you can hear, and the snorkel lets it wade water that would flood the Land Rover or the Troop Carrier, though the current can still take it. It starts with the episode's load, like the others. Its driving lamps come on with high beam. The LC79 V8 is the modern one: a current LandCruiser 79 single cab built up hard, with a 4 inch lift on coils, 37 inch mud tyres, a steel bar with a winch, driving lamps and a light bar, a snorkel up the right pillar, and an alloy tray with a black canopy, the recovery boards and a spare on its roof rack, and the other spare on the canopy's rear door. The canopy stops short of the tray's end so the spare stays inside the tray. Its tuned twin-turbo V8 diesel (about 200 kW and 750 Nm) makes it by far the quickest, about 4½ s to 60 km/h, and you can hear it: a deep V8 burble, the turbos whistling as they spool, a whoosh when you lift off, and a fuel-cut chop with turbo dump if you bounce it off the limiter (v0.89). The short-wheelbase petrol six keeps its hard-revving character and, at redline, adds an ignition-cut stutter, intake roar and pops if you lift off still wound out. It is thirsty, though: on the CREB it needs the jerry can. At 3 t on a 3.18 m wheelbase it is heavy and long, and the tray hangs out behind the rear wheels, but the wide track keeps it on its wheels in a full-lock turn at 50 km/h, and its snorkel head is the highest of the 4WDs.

**Frame rate (v1.49).** Pause menu → Settings → **Frame rate** picks **60** (the default) or **Screen**. The physics runs 60 times a second, so on 60 the picture is drawn at most about that often, which keeps a 120 Hz phone from drawing every frame twice and running hot; **Screen** draws on every refresh, for a strong computer with a fast monitor. `?bench` always draws every refresh.

**Manual gearbox (v1.35, controller only).** Settings → **Gearbox** picks **Automatic** (the default: the gearbox changes gear itself, as always) or **Manual** (you change gear and the clutch is worked for you). The row shows only while a controller is connected on a computer; the keyboard and touch always drive automatic, and if the setting is Manual but no controller is connected the car drives automatic. In Manual **D-pad right** changes up and **D-pad left** down, one step at a time: R ← N ← 1 ← 2 …; reverse is the step below neutral, R2 is the throttle in the selected gear and L2 only brakes (it never reverses). D-pad left/right no longer move the camera then (scroll still does). The car arrives in neutral, and R2 starts the engine in any gear (v1.37). The 40 Series is an automatic: in Manual you hold its gear (a 1-2-D lever). Precision crawl works in Manual too. **Manual with clutch** (v1.35–v1.36, ○/B held as the clutch) is shelved: a controller button made a poor clutch, and it will come back with steering-wheel and pedal support. A save that had it opens as Manual. Engine braking holds in a low gear down the descents.

The **tool menu** holds: Unhitch the trailer (while towing one), Winch (or Detach winch), the front and rear diff locks, Place planks (or Recall planks), precision crawl, the headlights, the repair the car needs (Field repair, Plug the tyre or Fit a spare) and Pour in a jerry can. Finds load by themselves when you stop beside them. An option that can't be used now is greyed out; picking it says why ("Stop first", "No spares left"...).

Placing planks: choose **Place planks** in the tool menu, move them with W/S, turn them with A/D, place with E, cancel with Esc. Prompts switch between keyboard and gamepad glyphs depending on what you last touched.

The **HUD** (v0.90, the Dash cluster) is a small group of analog dials: speedometer with the gear in a window, TEMP, FUEL (litres underneath on a computer) and a clock with the dark hours marked red on its rim. They sit bottom left on a computer and top left on a phone, with a ball compass at the top. Short **warning lamps** beside the dials show what is on (LOW, F LOCK, R LOCK, CRAWL, BRAKE, LIGHTS or HIGH BEAM, WINCH, TRAIL for let-down tyres) and what is wrong (STALLED, FLOODING, TEMP HIGH, ENGINE HOT, FUEL LOW, FLAT or TYRE per corner, engine and body damage, trailer trouble); a clean drive shows none. The road or course name and the mission line sit next to the dials. History and lesson notes come up small in the top right corner, under the Discovered card (v1.02). The **pause menu** sits on the right and starts with the time, fuel and jerry cans, spares, plugs and planks, and the tyres; when a new version has downloaded, a **Restart for the new version** button appears under Main menu. From v0.92, Settings → **HUD style** (title or pause) picks one of three looks, saved and applied at once: **Dash cluster** (the default, above); **Edge lines**, with no dials at all: a speed figure, thin TEMP and FUEL scales and the time along the bottom edge on a computer (along the top on a phone), and a compass tape at the top; and **Field kit**, a typed paper slip with the speed and gear, two chrome TEMP and FUEL gauges, a brass compass whose card turns, and a field watch with the dark hours shaded, in the bottom corners on a computer and the top corners on a phone, with label-tape warning lamps and a notebook pause menu. All three keep the menus on the right and the history notes small in the top right.

## Driving tips

- The engine is off when you arrive at the start of an episode, after restarting from a camp and after changing vehicle. Press the accelerator: the starter turns it over, and the car moves off once it fires. A stalled engine drags on the wheels through the gearing, so a dead car holds on a slope far better in low range and a low gear than in high (v1.19).
- **Low range (L)** and **precision crawl (P)** for the climbs and descents: hold W for a steady walking pace, let go and it brakes to a stop. On Big Red and Bob's Hill lock both diffs (**Z**, **X**) below 5 km/h.
- The red clay is slick after rain: brake early, keep a steady throttle (spinning the wheels digs you in), and lock both diffs in low range before you start up any steep pinch, not halfway up it.
- Take the mud holes steadily, in a low gear, without stopping in them.
- At the **washout** stop short of the gap, square up, choose **Place planks** and slide them out until they bridge it under the front wheels, then crawl over in low range. Recall them from the tool menu once you are well past. The Unimog's big wheels climb straight through it.
- Crawl up the **rock ledge** in low range with both lockers. Spinning the wheels on the rock cuts the tyres.
- On the **sideling** crawl, keep to the high side and don't turn downhill: taken fast on the wet clay, the car slides towards the drop.
- The **Bloomfield River** at Wujal Wujal is a timber bridge with boards missing after the wet. Crawl it (L and P, or 4WD → CRAWL on a phone) and steer for the timber you can see: first keep left of a hole, then right of one, then straddle a gap down the middle. Off the boards you drop into the river; pause and restart from the camp. It is too deep to ford.
- Slow right down before the **creeks** on the Bloomfield Track and keep moving once you are in: stopped in the current, the car is pushed downstream into the scour hole below the middle one.
- **C** cycles the views; the front views look across the tyres to place a wheel, the driver's seat has working dials. **I** shows the crawl gauges (pitch, roll and the load on each tyre).
- In deep water watch the air intake: water at the intake stalls the engine (with Realistic damage it can wreck it). Cross the Daintree on the gravel bar in low range with both lockers, weaving between its boulders and the darker water of its holes, and keep moving, steering upstream against the current: stop on the bar and the current slowly pushes the car off it, and taken quickly in high range the short-wheelbase 4WD is pushed off it into the deep water. Stopped in deep running water, the car is lifted and carried off: "Deep, fast water" warns you, "The current is taking the car" means get to shallow water now.
- On the gravel roads about 30–40 km/h is a fair pace, and less on the clay. Faster, the swells bounce the car and the potholes and washouts (the dark ones ringed with pale gravel) pinch tyres.
- Stay on the track: the dark ground off it is black soil, and bogs you. Cutting a corner onto it costs minutes.
- **Winching someone else out:** stop on firm ground facing them, hook on and winch in. The handbrake holds your car while you winch (v1.19: it used to creep towards them in reverse); if you're light and it's wet you'll slide a little towards them, so leave some room. Let go of the winch once they're out.
- The winch reaches any tree or marked rock within about 30 m. Rainforest trees are everywhere on the range, a tree stands near each end of every deep or long bog, and marked rocks stand along Big Red just right of the crown. Bogged in a deep one, or stalled on the climb, hook up to the tree or rock ahead (E / USE), winch in (R / IN) and drive gently with it. With more than one anchor in reach, E / USE shows them all (v1.18): faint rings on each, a bright ring and a dashed line to the picked one, and its name and distance in the prompt. Step through them with A / D (the stick on a phone, the D-pad on a pad), or tap or click one, then press E / USE again (or tap it again) to hook it; Esc cancels.
- **Soft sand** (the pale ground in the dry riverbeds) drags the car and lets the wheels dig in. Take it steadily in a low gear and keep moving: stopped in it, the car has to climb out of its own ruts.
- **Termite mounds** are as hard as rock. Steer round them off the road.
- At a fork, the sign names the other line. The short line is the quicker one if you drive it well; the long way round is there for when it's going badly, or the river is up.
- A tree down across the track: stop well short of it, on the side its roots are, and pick **Fallen tree (crown end)** with the tool menu's Winch (or press E and step to it: with more than one anchor in reach, E opens the same chooser). Winch in on the handbrake, holding the brake so the car doesn't creep towards the tree, and the crown swings round towards you; stop once it's round, or the winch drags the whole tree towards you; if it still lies across, back up a few metres and pull again. Then back off and drive past on the other side.
- Keep an eye on the clock once it rains: the creeks only get higher. In a risen creek, a steady pace in high range can get the short-wheelbase 4WD across where crawling lets the current take it.
- Got a flat? Stop and fix it: a flat tyre pulls the car off line and makes the corners treacherous.

## Test mode

Add `?dev` to the address: https://reidstephen11.github.io/over-the-cape/?dev. Every episode is open on the welcome screen, with a choice of time of day and weather for any of them. In the game a **Test mode** panel at the top right lets you (click its title to fold it away):

- set the clock (the light follows) and stop it; there is no time limit;
- set the weather: clear, cloudy with wet ground, light rain or a heavy storm, then fine-tune the rain, how wet the ground is and how high the rivers are;
- start from any place on the map;
- change vehicle where you are;
- hitch a 6×4 box trailer behind the car, empty or with two 200 L drums of diesel, or take it away (from v0.46; test mode is now the only place trailers appear); behind the Land Rover or the Unimog, the Army's No. 5 trailer instead (from v0.49);
- go back to the welcome screen to change episode.

**Towing a trailer** (test mode only). The trailer rides on the tow ball (the pintle hook on the Land Rover and the Unimog) and follows on its own two wheels; it has no brakes, so a loaded one makes the car slower to pull away and to stop. The camera stands further back while it's on. Reverse gently: on full lock it folds round until the drawbar is against the back of the car (the HUD says it has jackknifed), and you pull forward to straighten it. A loaded trailer leans hard in corners and can tip over in a fast turn across a slope. Stopped, **Unhitch the trailer** (tool menu) stands it on its jockey wheel with its wheels chocked, so you can drive or winch a hard bit without it; back up until the ball is under the coupling (within about 0.7 m), stop and press **E** (A on a pad, USE on a phone) to hitch it again. A trailer standing on its own can be winched by its drawbar. From v0.48 the HUD shows it beside the jerry cans: how many drums are on board and what the trailer weighs with them (amber when it's over its 750 kg limit or jackknifed, red when it's over on its side), or how far away you left it. It rattles over rough ground (less with drums in it, which boom instead), bangs on hard hits, and clanks as the coupling goes on or off the ball. From v0.49 there are two trailers, both drawn from photographs: the galvanised 6×4 box trailer, and the Army's No. 5 in flat green, with its spare wheel on the drawbar and a lunette ring that drops over the pintle hook, so it goes only behind the Land Rover or the Unimog (change to another vehicle while towing it and it's left standing). The No. 5 is heavier (360 kg empty, 500 kg of cargo) and sits on a narrow track with a high floor: loaded, it goes over more easily than the box trailer in a turn across a slope, and reversed hard into a jackknife behind the Unimog it can tip.

Nothing is saved in test mode, so your real progress is safe. Puddles on the track come from the weather picked on the welcome screen. There is no wind yet.

## Deployment

`.github/workflows/deploy.yml` runs lint and the type check, the unit tests (split across three runners with Vitest's `--shard`, since v1.45) and the production build on every pull request and on every push to `main`. A push to `main` then deploys `dist/` to GitHub Pages (**Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions**).
